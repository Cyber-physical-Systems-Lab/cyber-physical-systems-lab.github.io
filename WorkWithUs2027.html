Want to Work With Us? PhD Positions and Our 2027 Research Internship

Post's content provided by: Didem Gürdür Broo

Every month we receive many emails from students around the world who would like to do a PhD in our lab. Thank you. It is genuinely encouraging to see how many of you are excited about multi-robot systems, multi-agent learning and nature-inspired robotics. This page answers the two questions we are asked most often: how to become a PhD student here, and how to work with us right now, even when no PhD position is open.

1. PhD positions: please apply through an open call

At Uppsala University, a PhD position is a funded, salaried job. Every position is advertised publicly and filled through open, competitive recruitment. This means we can only consider PhD applications that are submitted to an open call, through the university's application system.

Please do not email us a PhD application when there is no open call. We are not able to assess or keep unsolicited applications, and sending one does not give an advantage when a position opens. We read every message we receive, but we cannot reply to each one individually.

When we have an open PhD position, you will find it in these three places:

  • CPS-Lab Open Positions
    Our own list of current and past calls.
  • Uppsala University Vacancies
    Where you submit the actual application.
  • Didem Gürdür Broo on LinkedIn
    Where new calls are announced first.

Our advice: follow these pages, read our recent papers, and have your CV, transcripts and a short research statement ready. When a call opens, you can then apply well before the deadline.

2. Work with us now: Research Internship Programme 2027

If you want to experience research in our lab before any PhD call opens, our Research Internship Programme is the way to do it. The internship is online and part-time, so it costs you nothing to take part: no travel, no relocation, no fees. For about four and a half months you will work in a small team on a real research question, with mentoring from our researchers, and see first-hand how we work.

  • When:
    1 February – 15 June 2027
  • Format:
    Online, part-time (about 8–10 h/week)
  • Places:
    6 interns in two teams of three
  • Deadline:
    15 December 2026

Interns meet as a cohort online every two weeks, present their progress mid-term in April, and give a final presentation in June. Each team works on one of the two projects below, which build directly on our published research. Each intern owns one subtask, and the team shares one codebase.

Project A: Symbiotic cooperation in heterogeneous multi-robot teams

In a team of robots with different capabilities, one robot's action often helps or hinders another. We model these directed effects with ideas from biological symbiosis (mutualism, commensalism, parasitism) and build them into multi-agent reinforcement learning. The starting point is a simulated warehouse where heterogeneous mobile robots coordinate deliveries and battery charging.

  • A1, Scaling up: extend the warehouse scenario from two robots to larger teams with more kinds of heterogeneity, and test whether sharing battery state still improves cooperation.
  • A2, Benchmarking: compare symbiosis-inspired reward shaping with standard cooperative MARL baselines (such as VDN, QMIX and MAPPO) on weakly and strongly coupled tasks.
  • A3, Measuring symbiosis: build tools that quantify each robot's effect on the others during an episode, and test whether these measures explain team performance.

Key reading:

  • X. Niu, N. Calvo Barajas and D. Gürdür Broo, Enabling Symbiosis in Multi-Robot Systems Through Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning, IEEE ICPS 2025 (blog post).
  • X. Niu and D. Gürdür Broo, Investigating Symbiosis in Robotic Ecosystems: A Case Study for Multi-Robot Reinforcement Learning Reward Shaping, ICRAS 2025.

Project B: Self-organising coordination in open multi-agent systems

Real robot teams are open: robots join, leave or fail, and tasks appear and disappear while the system runs. Our MORPH framework lets robots adapt which teammates they coordinate with, using a rewiring mechanism inspired by neuroplasticity, so the team's coordination structure reorganises itself for task allocation. This project tests how far that self-organisation holds up as the system becomes more open and less connected.

  • B1, Openness stress tests: build scenarios where robot arrivals, departures, failures and new tasks can each be varied independently, and measure how well MORPH recovers.
  • B2, Baselines: compare MORPH with classical decentralised task allocation (such as auction-based methods and CBBA) and a MARL baseline.
  • B3, Communication limits: study how coordination degrades under limited range, delays or message loss, and whether the adaptive topology compensates.

Key reading:

  • X. Niu and D. Gürdür Broo, MORPH: Self-Organising Multi-Robot Task Allocation via Neuroplasticity-Inspired Adaptive Topology, arXiv preprint. Code: github.com/Cyber-physical-Systems-Lab/morph.

What you get

  • Hands-on experience in an active robotics research group.
  • Mentoring from a PhD student or postdoc and from the lab director.
  • A concrete research output: a joint technical report or workshop-paper draft per team, with your code in the lab repository.
  • A certificate of completion and, for strong work, a reference letter. Co-authorship where your contribution warrants it.

Please note

  • The internship is unpaid and is not employment at Uppsala University.
  • It does not give academic credit.
  • It is not a route to a PhD position. PhD positions are always filled through open calls, as described above.

Who can apply

  • Students enrolled in a Master's programme (or the final year of a Bachelor's) in computer science, electrical engineering, robotics, applied mathematics or a related field, or people who graduated in 2026. Current PhD students may also apply.
  • Good programming skills in Python.
  • Experience with reinforcement learning, robotics or simulation.
  • Ability to work independently and communicate in English.

How to apply

Send one PDF to didem.gurdur.broo@it.uu.se and xuezhi.niu@it.uu.se by 15 December 2026, containing:

  1. A CV (at most 4 pages).
  2. A motivation letter (at most 1,000 words) saying which project (A or B) and which subtask you prefer, and why.

We assess applications on motivation, relevant skills and fit with the two projects. Shortlisted applicants are invited to an interview and receive a short task to complete beforehand. We aim to send decisions in mid-January 2027.

Thank you

We know that writing to a lab takes courage and effort, and we appreciate every message. Whether you join us as an intern this spring or apply to a future PhD call, we hope to see your name again. Keep building, keep reading, and keep in touch through the pages above.
